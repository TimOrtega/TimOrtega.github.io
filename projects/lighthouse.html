Lighthouse assembly

Turning flat parts into a structure through joints, fit, and fastening.

SolidWorksLaser-cut MDFMechanical fastening
The story

I built this lighthouse using laser-cut MDF, 3D-printed components, and standard screws and nuts. The challenge was to design parts that would locate, interlock, and fasten together as an assembly, rather than treating each panel as an isolated shape.

Project focus
  • T joints & lap joints
  • Press-fit interfaces
  • Screws, nuts & panel alignment
Explore 3D models ↓
01 / From panels to structure

Think in flat parts.
Design in three dimensions.

Laser cutting starts with sheet material, but the finished lighthouse depends on how those sheets relate in space. I used SolidWorks to plan the assembly and bring together the MDF panels, printed components, screws, and nuts.

The project combined subtractive fabrication, additive fabrication, and purchased fasteners. It made assembly order, access to hardware, and the alignment of neighboring parts part of the design problem.

02 / Joint design

The joints make
the assembly.

I used T joints, lap joints, and press-fit joints to connect and locate parts. Each joint controls how pieces meet: tabs and slots establish position, overlapping geometry connects panels, and fit helps parts hold their relationship during assembly.

Screws and nuts provide mechanical fastening alongside the interlocking MDF geometry. Designing around sheet thickness, laser-cut kerf, and part clearances connects the CAD model to how the physical assembly fits together.
