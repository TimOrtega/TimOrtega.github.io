Rocketry drill jig

Designing the tool that makes a repeatable manufacturing process possible.

Penn Aerospace ClubHigh Power RocketrySept 2026–present
The story

On Penn Aerospace Club’s High Power Rocketry Team, I designed, fabricated, and tested a drill jig for drilling mounting holes into the rocket’s fiberglass airframe. The objective was precise, repeatable angular spacing from one hole to the next.

Project focus
  • Repeatable angular spacing
  • Reusable jig body
  • Replaceable drill-contact components
Explore 3D models ↓
01 / The manufacturing problem

Repeatability starts
with the fixture.

Mounting holes in a cylindrical airframe need consistent angular spacing. A drill jig turns that geometric requirement into a physical reference for the drilling operation, helping locate successive holes in a repeatable way.

My work covered the design, fabrication, and testing of that jig. The project connected CAD, part interfaces, and the practical demands of using a tool on an actual fiberglass airframe.

02 / Modular design

Keep the body.
Replace the contact part.

I engineered the jig as a reusable main body paired with small, low-cost, easily reproducible drill-contact components. Separating those functions reduced material cost and waste while maintaining accuracy.

This was a design-for-maintenance decision: the larger body provides the locating geometry, while a smaller replaceable insert handles the drill-contact interface. The interface between them is as important as either part on its own.

Engineering in practice

Design the process,
not only the part.

The jig is a tool for producing another component. Designing it meant thinking about repeatability, locating features, replaceable interfaces, and how material is consumed during fabrication.
